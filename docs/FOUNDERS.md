# The minds behind the $42M: a dossier

Researched 16 August 2026 from their whitepaper, papers, interviews, talks
and biography coverage. Written to answer one question: when these three
read an application, what are they hoping to find?

## The three minds

**Sandeep Nailwal** -- born 1987 in Ramnagar, a Himalayan farming village
with no electricity, to illiterate teenage parents; raised in Jamna-Paar,
the east-bank Delhi settlement the city treats as its wrong side; father
an alcoholic; working since sixth grade; the child kept standing outside
the classroom because the fees were unpaid. Failed the IIT entrance exam,
pivoted, MBA, quit the corporate track, co-founded Polygon -- the network
that made Ethereum affordable to people priced out of it. Ran the COVID
Crypto Relief Fund: turned Vitalik's billion-dollar SHIB donation into
$474M without crashing the market, moved $74M into India's emergency, and
bought 160 million syringes through UNICEF in days. The pattern is the
man: infrastructure for the excluded, execution as an act of compassion,
speed as respect. His stated frame for Sentient: crypto fixes AI's
centralization, and India proves it can define the AI future, not follow
it.

**Himanshu Tyagi** -- IISc Bengaluru professor, information theorist, the
co-founder who runs the build. His sharpest public line calls closed AI
the greatest heist of public knowledge in modern history: the labs
trained on humanity's commons, then locked the door and sold tickets. His
geopolitics: unless India and nations like it build their own advanced
AI, they are locked out of the future economy permanently. His economics:
idealism loses unless open source gets a business model -- monetization is
the existential question, and crypto incentives are his answer.

**Pramod Viswanath** -- Princeton professor, one of the defining figures
of information theory (the standard wireless textbook is half his), now
building what he calls trust systems. His lab wrote the OML papers
(NeurIPS 2025) and the fingerprinting mechanisms: the mathematics that
lets a model be given away and still owned. He supplies the discipline:
every value the project holds must ultimately be a *mechanism*, not a
sentiment.

## The creed, distilled

1. **Intelligence is a commons that was enclosed.** Models are built from
   humanity's shared knowledge; closing them is theft from everyone. This
   is not rhetoric to them -- it is the founding grievance.
2. **Openness must be mechanized, or it loses.** Their whole stack -- OML,
   fingerprinting, staked enforcement -- exists because open-and-broke
   loses to closed-and-rich. An applicant who says "open" without a
   sustainability mechanism has not understood them.
3. **Loyalty is the product.** Their word, their paper: AI where the
   community has ownership, alignment and control. The model answers to
   the person it serves, not to whoever trained it.
4. **The default user is the excluded one.** The cheapest phone, the
   person outside the classroom, the grandmother taking the scam call.
   Sandeep's childhood is the program's persona.
5. **India -- and the skipped world -- defines, not follows.** Talent is
   everywhere; rails are not; they are building rails.
6. **Execution is moral.** The syringes story. "Real builders with real
   repos move fast. So will we." Slowness and vaporware are, to them,
   ethical failures.
7. **Merit over pedigree.** The founder who failed IIT reviews on merit
   and deliberately reaches into underserved markets. No credential
   theatre required; measured results required absolutely.

## How they will read an application

- First the repo, then the claims. A runnable demo outranks a deck.
- They pattern-match for their own stack: extending EvoSkill, ROMA, OML,
  or the enclave work signals fluency and reduces their risk.
- They flinch at: closed components with no open load-bearing part;
  AGI-hype language without mechanisms; US-only framing; education-market
  cliches; anything that reads as harvesting users rather than arming
  them.
- They lean in at: loyalty made concrete; on-device and offline as
  dignity, not just latency; a founder whose life story is the user's
  story; numbers with instruments behind them; speed.

## The synthesis that matters for us

Sentient solved loyalty for **weights**: fingerprinting lets a model be
released and still owned. Nobody has solved loyalty for **behaviour** --
what an agent learns after deployment lives in opaque context windows or
closed fine-tunes. Nyaya's learned-skills-as-readable-programs is exactly
that missing layer: cognition the user can read, edit, carry, and own.
OML for what the agent *learns*. Pitched in their language: the loyal
behaviour layer, extending EvoSkill with the OML philosophy applied to
skills. That is the sentence this dossier exists to earn.
